# Solution card

One card per solution, whether found elsewhere or built in the cycle. Only the last two fields depend on the place.
That is what makes a card from one city searchable by another.

| Field | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Need** | What do people need, without naming the place? | |
| **Principle** | What mechanism solves it? | |
| **Conditions** | Climate, resources, materials, income, infrastructure, culture, governance, scale | |
| **Local form** | What did it look like there? | |
| **Adoption** | Why did it take hold, or why didn't it? | |
| **Coordinates** | Where, when, and did it arise on its own or come from elsewhere? | |

**Origin:** the card this one adapts (link), or "new".
**Authors:** who wrote this card.
**Sources:** links to where the solution is documented.

---

## Example: the bow

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Need** | Reach food at a distance, safely |
| **Principle** | Store energy slowly and release it all at once |
| **Conditions** | Flexible wood, fiber for the string, mobile hunting |
| **Local form** | Each people's own bow, made from local materials |
| **Adoption** | It spread wherever hunting at a distance paid off |
| **Coordinates** | Many places and periods |

## Example: cooling a street without expensive energy

The principles are shade, cross ventilation, and evaporation. A team starts from what already exists and fills in one
card for each form:
- a perforated block wall that lets air through while blocking the sun, such as the Brazilian *cobogó*;
- green corridors of trees along streets and waterways;
- light-colored roofs that reflect heat.

Then the team compares each card's conditions with its own neighborhood's before adapting.
