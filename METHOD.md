# The Campwe method · version 0.1

A way for a city to solve its own problems: **the neighborhood names the problem, university students build
solutions with the people who live it, and the city tests the ones that advance.** Everything stays public, credited
to whoever made it, and any other city can adapt it.

This is version 0.1: the method written down before the first complete cycle. Anything not yet tested is marked
**(to be tested)**.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **The door is open to everyone.** Anyone can raise a problem and follow what was done with it.
2. **The problem belongs to the neighborhood.** The challenge comes out of listening, never out of a ready-made agenda.
3. **The author moves the idea forward.** Nobody promotes or discards someone else's idea without saying why.
4. **Credit is recorded.** Every idea has an author, a date, and a history of who contributed what.
5. **Global problem, local solution.** An idea can be adapted to another place, and the adaptation always points
   back to its origin.
6. **Sponsors don't own ideas.** Companies and foundations support the cycle and receive the report, but the ideas
   stay public.
7. **Everything goes back to the street.** A cycle only ends when the neighborhood learns what happened to what it
   said.

---

## 2. The building: three floors

The method is a path through three floors. What defines it is not each floor, but the passage from one to the next.

| Floor | What happens | Who is there | What moves up to the next floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st · Listening** | The problem is told | Residents, local shops, neighborhood services | One clear question |
| **2nd · Challenge** | Ideas emerge | Student teams with a faculty advisor, working with the people who live the problem | Ideas with versions and credit |
| **3rd · Implementation** | The idea becomes a test and a project | Local government, sponsors, the idea's team | The test result, which goes back to the neighborhood |

---

## 3. Roles

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Cycle organizer** | Runs the listening, writes the challenge, operates the idea registry, organizes the final, and writes the report. Does not pick the ideas: looks after the path |
| **Educational institution** | Registers the challenge in its own system (in Brazilian universities, as an extension project, the community engagement every degree must include), appoints a coordinator and one faculty advisor per team, and certifies student hours under its own rules |
| **Local government** | Helps choose the neighborhood, names a contact person, provides space, and assesses with the organizer the test of the ideas that advance |
| **Residents** | Tell the problem, work with the teams, and say what they think of the ideas |
| **Teams** | Students from one or more programs and one or more institutions, each team with an advisor |
| **Sponsor** (optional) | Pays part of the operation or the test. Receives the report and the contact details of those who agreed to be contacted. Owns no idea |

When several institutions join together, the cycle has a **committee** with one coordinator from each, the organizer,
and local government. The same neighborhood and the same challenge apply to all. Each institution certifies its own
students, and institutions are not ranked against each other.

---

## 4. The six-month cycle

The cycle fits in one academic semester, so the challenge fits into the universities' extension calendar.

| Month | Stage | What happens | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Listening** | Open meetings in the neighborhood. People who live and work there say what needs to change | The listening sheet and the challenge question ([template](templates/listening.md)) |
| 2 | **Launch** | The challenge is published, institutions register the project, and teams form | The challenge sheet, with the final's criteria published in advance ([template](templates/challenge.md)) |
| 3 | **Field** | Teams go to the neighborhood with their advisors and work with the people who live the problem | Each team's field notes |
| 4 | **Ideas** | Ideas and their versions go public. The city supports them and suggests improvements | Published ideas, with author and history |
| 5 | **Final** | A final presentation open to the city, with attendance recorded | The list of ideas that move on to testing |
| 6 | **Report** | The public report states the outcome of each idea, and the ones that advanced move up to the 3rd floor | The cycle report ([template](templates/report.md)) |

Testing the ideas that advance happens after the cycle, with local government. It may take longer than one semester
**(to be tested)**.

---

## 5. The idea registry

Each idea is recorded as a project with a history:

| What a person does | What gets recorded |
|---|---|
| Writes the idea in one sentence | The idea is born, with author, date, and place |
| Improves their own idea | A new version, with what changed |
| Proposes an improvement to someone else's idea | A proposal, which only the author accepts or declines |
| Adapts the idea to another place | A **derivative**, which points to the origin and never erases it |
| Supports it | A support, with a name |

Who contributed what can be reconstructed at any time. That is what makes real credit possible in the report and the
certificate.

**People can withdraw their personal data.** The idea stays recorded, and the credit becomes anonymous.

---

## 6. The rules that don't change

They apply to every cycle and every version of the method. A new version may improve the path, never break these
rules.

1. **The problem belongs to the neighborhood.** The challenge comes out of listening.
2. **Ideas are public, with credit.** Nobody owns an idea from the challenge: not the organizer, not the sponsor,
   not the institution.
3. **No ranking of people and no large cash prize.** The final's criteria are agreed with the institutions and
   published in advance.
4. **Each party certifies what is theirs.** Extension hours are certified by the educational institution. The
   organizer certifies only what it measures: attendance at the final and contribution to ideas.
5. **Every idea has a declared outcome.** Adopted, adapted, or archived, and why.

---

## 7. The cycle report

The report is the proof that the cycle happened. It is public and states:

- who was heard during listening (how many people, from where, without exposing anyone who chose not to appear);
- the challenge question and why it was chosen;
- how many teams, programs, and institutions took part;
- the ideas and their versions, with each person's credit;
- who attended the final;
- **the outcome of each idea: adopted, adapted, or archived, and why**;
- what changes in the next cycle.

## 8. How to adapt it for your city

This method is free (see the [license](LICENSE)). To run a cycle:

1. Choose a neighborhood and a problem with your local government.
2. Find an educational institution that will register the challenge in its system.
3. Follow the cycle in §4 and the [templates](templates/).
4. Publish the report.

When you adapt it, say where it came from: "based on the Campwe method," with a link to this repository. If you
change the method and distribute your version, it must keep the same license. Tell us what you changed: open an
issue in this repository or register your version in [DERIVATIVES.md](DERIVATIVES.md).

The Campwe name is not covered by the license. See [TRADEMARK.md](TRADEMARK.md).

## 9. What we don't know yet (to be tested in the first cycle)

- How many listening meetings are enough for a good question.
- Whether teams mixing students from different institutions work better or worse.
- How long the neighborhood test needs after the cycle.
- How much of the operation local government can take over on its own from the second cycle on.
